# Module 0 · Basics

The four plain things most other cooking leans on: a pot of rice, a pot of pasta, a boiled egg and a green vegetable. Each is cooked in water, and each goes wrong in a different way, so each lesson practises a different control. Rice is about measuring, because nothing is drained and the water you put in is the water the rice ends up holding. Pasta is about tasting, because the packet time is only a guess about your pot. Eggs are about timing, because you cannot see inside the shell. Green beans are about stopping, because a vegetable keeps cooking after it leaves the water unless you cool it.

These are also four of the most rule-bound jobs in a kitchen, and several of the rules do not survive testing. The lessons say which. Salt does not make pasta water boil usefully hotter. A huge pot is not needed. Leaving the lid off does not keep vegetables green; a lot of salt does. Eggs peel well when they go into boiling water, not when they start cold.

The other modules assume you can do these four things. If you can, skip ahead; nothing later depends on having read this.

## The lessons

1. [Plain rice](1%20Plain%20rice.cook) uses the absorption method: rinse, one and a half times the rice's volume of water, lid on, then a rest.
2. [Buttered pasta](2%20Buttered%20pasta.cook) is pasta in salted boiling water with only butter and cheese on it, so you can taste whether the pasta itself is right.
3. [Boiled eggs, soft to hard](3%20Boiled%20eggs,%20soft%20to%20hard.cook) cooks six eggs and takes them out in pairs, so you can calibrate your own hob, pot and eggs.
4. [Blanched green beans](4%20Blanched%20green%20beans.cook) is blanching and shocking: a short boil in very salty water, then ice.

## What you need

A saucepan with a tight lid, a large pot, a sieve, a slotted spoon, a timer and ice.

## Safety

The soft-yolked eggs in lesson 3 are below official advice, which is to cook eggs until yolk and white are firm; the lesson says how to meet it. Cooked rice has to be cooled and stored with care, and lesson 1 gives the rule.
