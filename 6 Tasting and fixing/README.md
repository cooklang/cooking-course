# Module 6 · Tasting and fixing

Every recipe ends with "season to taste" and almost none says how. This module is about that sentence. The method is the same in all four lessons: taste, change one thing by a small amount, and taste again. The lessons are built so that the difference is easy to notice, starting with a leaf bitter enough that a few grains of salt visibly change it.

Tastes are not independent dials. In a mixture each one is perceived as weaker than it would be alone, and they suppress each other unevenly: salt turns bitterness down, but bitterness does not turn saltiness down. Adjusting a dish means using one taste to move another. The lessons work through the main tools in turn: salt, which makes what is already there stronger and quietens bitterness; acid, which balances and brightens; umami, the savoury taste of glutamate, which some ingredients multiply when combined; and fat, which softens sharpness. Thickness matters too: a sauce that is too thick tastes duller, and may need thinning before it needs seasoning.

The last lesson cooks a stew with no salt and no acid on purpose and then fixes it in a fixed order. That order, salt, acid, fat, sweet, is this course's working convention and not a research finding, and the lesson says so. It also lists what to do when a dish has gone too far, what does not work (the potato in the pot), and where the limits are: these fixes mask a mild excess, not a serious one.

## The lessons

1. [Bitter leaf salad](1%20Bitter%20leaf%20salad.cook) tastes the same leaf with and without salt.
2. [Lentils with lemon](2%20Lentils%20with%20lemon.cook) cooks lentils plain, then seasons them in two moves: salt, then acid.
3. [Tomato pasta, tuned](3%20Tomato%20pasta,%20tuned.cook) adds depth with umami and softens sharpness with fat.
4. [Chickpea stew, fixed](4%20Chickpea%20stew,%20fixed.cook) puts the module together: one change at a time, and the rescues.

## What you need

A saucepan, a large pot, a large bowl and a spoon you keep tasting with. No special equipment.
