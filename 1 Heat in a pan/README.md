# Module 1 · Heat in a pan

A frying pan is the fastest way to brown food, and browning is where a great deal of flavour comes from. This module is about what stands in its way, which is nearly always water. Water cannot rise above 100 °C until it has turned to vapour, and turning it to vapour takes a lot of energy, so a wet surface stays near 100 °C while the reactions that brown food only run quickly well above that. Every lesson here is a different way of getting water out of the way: giving it time, driving it off on purpose, starting dry and hot, or cooking in batches small enough for the pan to keep up.

The four dishes also disagree with each other usefully. The chicken starts in a cold pan and the steak in a very hot one, and both are right, because one is rendering fat out of skin and the other is searing. The mushrooms are crowded into the pan with water on purpose; the stir-fry is cooked in batches so that it never is.

The steak lesson carries the module's main piece of chemistry, the Maillard reaction, step by step and by name.

## The lessons

1. [Crispy chicken thighs](1%20Crispy%20chicken%20thighs.cook) renders the fat from chicken skin by starting it in a cold pan.
2. [Mushrooms on toast](2%20Mushrooms%20on%20toast.cook) gets the water out of mushrooms first and browns them second.
3. [Steak with pan sauce](3%20Steak%20with%20pan%20sauce.cook) sears a steak, rests it, and makes a sauce from what the sear leaves stuck to the pan.
4. [Any-vegetable stir-fry](4%20Any-vegetable%20stir-fry.cook) puts the module together, with ingredients you choose.

## What you need

A large frying pan, a second pan of plain stainless steel or cast iron for the steak (non-stick will not build the layer the sauce is made from), a wooden spoon and a thermometer.

## Safety

The chicken and the stir-fry give the official minimum temperatures for poultry, pork and fish. The steak is cooked to the USDA figure of 63 °C, and its note says what choosing a pinker steak means.
