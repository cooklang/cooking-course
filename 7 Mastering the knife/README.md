# Module 7 · Mastering the knife

Most of the time spent cooking is spent cutting, and most recipes assume you can. This module teaches the handful of things that make cutting safe, even and eventually quick: how to hold the knife, how to hold the food, how to make something round sit still, and how to cut to a chosen size. Speed is not a goal here. Accuracy is, and speed follows it.

There are two hands to learn. The knife hand pinches the blade where it meets the handle, which gives control over the whole length of the knife. The other hand holds the food in a claw, fingertips tucked back, so the knuckles guide the blade and set the width of each slice. Everything else builds on those: the onion dice, the rocking motion for mincing, the plank-then-stick-then-dice sequence that works for any firm vegetable.

How you cut also changes how food tastes and cooks. Garlic gets stronger the more its cells are broken, so sliced, minced and pasted garlic are three different ingredients. Pieces of the same size cook at the same rate, so an uneven dice gives a pot that is half hard and half falling apart. A sharp knife needs less force and slips less.

A caution about the format: grips and cuts are described here in words, which is the weakest way to teach them. These lessons will gain video links before any others. Until then, read the step, go slowly, and look at what is on your board.

## The lessons

1. [Tomato salsa](1%20Tomato%20salsa.cook) teaches the pinch grip, the claw and dicing an onion.
2. [Chimichurri](2%20Chimichurri.cook) teaches mincing herbs and garlic, and how the cut sets the strength of garlic.
3. [Coleslaw](3%20Coleslaw.cook) teaches thin, even slices and matchsticks, and salting water out of cabbage.
4. [Diced vegetable soup](4%20Diced%20vegetable%20soup.cook) puts the module together: every cut in one pot, all to the same size.

## What you need

A sharp chef's knife, a chopping board, and a damp cloth to put under the board so it cannot slide.
