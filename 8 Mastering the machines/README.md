# Module 8 · Mastering the machines

Four appliances, one lesson each. They get a module of their own because each one is usually explained by its buttons, and each makes more sense once you know the one physical thing it does.

A slow cooker holds food just below a boil for hours with the lid on. That is the temperature band where tough cuts turn tender, and the sealed lid means almost nothing evaporates, so a sauce never reduces on its own. An air fryer is a small convection oven that circulates hot air around the food; everything that helps it is about letting that air reach every surface. A pressure cooker seals in steam so that water boils at about 121 °C and not 100 °C, and food cooks faster for that reason alone. A microwave heats only the outer couple of centimetres of food and relies on that heat travelling inwards, and its power setting does not weaken the waves, it switches them off for part of each minute.

Each lesson also says what its machine cannot do. A slow cooker cannot brown. An air fryer does not fry, and gives results closer to an oven than to a deep fryer. A pressure cooker cannot be checked while it works. A microwave cannot heat the middle of something thick quickly without overcooking the outside.

The lessons do not depend on each other. Cook the ones for the machines you own.

## The lessons

1. [Slow-cooker beef chilli](1%20Slow-cooker%20beef%20chilli.cook) browns in a pan first, uses little liquid, and leaves the lid alone.
2. [Air-fryer potato wedges](2%20Air-fryer%20potato%20wedges.cook) is about dry food, a little oil, space in the basket and checking early.
3. [Pressure-cooker risotto](3%20Pressure-cooker%20risotto.cook) cooks in six minutes under pressure, with the right liquid, fill level and release.
4. [Microwave jacket potatoes](4%20Microwave%20jacket%20potatoes.cook) uses half power, a turn and a rest.

## What you need

The appliance for each lesson, a frying pan for the chilli, and your appliance's manual for its fill lines and its pressure-release instructions.

## Safety

The slow-cooker lesson carries the USDA's rules: thaw meat first, keep the lid on, throw the food away if the power fails while you are out, and reheat leftovers on the hob or in a microwave, not in the slow cooker. Never fill a pressure cooker more than two-thirds, and keep hands and face away from the steam when you release it. The risotto lesson carries the rule for cooked rice.
