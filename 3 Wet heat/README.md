# Module 3 · Wet heat

Cooking in liquid has a ceiling. While there is water in the pot the food cannot go above 100 °C, so nothing browns, and the skill is in choosing how far below that ceiling to work. The module starts with the two gentlest techniques: sweating, which softens vegetables in their own moisture with no colour, and simmering, which the first lesson shows is a different temperature from boiling, about 82 to 88 °C against 100 °C, and not just a smaller flame.

The second half is about what long, gentle heat does to meat. Tough cuts are tough because of collagen, the protein in connective tissue. Held in a moist pot between roughly 60 and 93 °C for hours, collagen breaks down and the meat turns tender; in a stock, it dissolves out of bones and skin as gelatin, which is why good stock sets to jelly in the fridge. The stew lesson also clears up a common belief: the liquid does not make the meat moist. Braised meat loses water like any other; what changes is the collagen.

## The lessons

1. [Tomato soup](1%20Tomato%20soup.cook) teaches sweating and simmering, and seasoning at the end.
2. [Poached chicken and stock](2%20Poached%20chicken%20and%20stock.cook) cooks meat gently, takes it out when it is done, and gives the bones another hour and a half.
3. [Beef stew](3%20Beef%20stew.cook) is a braise: brown the meat, then keep it just below a simmer until a fork slides in.
4. [Any-bean soup](4%20Any-bean%20soup.cook) puts the module together, with ingredients you choose, added in order of how long they take.

## What you need

A large pot, a heavy casserole with a lid that can go from hob to oven, a stick blender, a sieve and a thermometer.

## Safety

Poultry is poached to 74 °C. The risk with stock and stew comes afterwards: both lessons say to cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate them.
